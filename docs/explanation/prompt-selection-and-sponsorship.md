# Prompt Selection and Sponsorship

## Community prompt pipeline

1. Users submit suggestions with category, content notes, accessibility
   information, and optional attribution.
2. Rate limits and basic validation reduce spam.
3. AI screens for unsafe content, sexual exploitation of minors, hate,
   harassment, illegal instructions, spam, and likely duplicates.
4. Similar suggestions are indexed and grouped into clusters. Similarity
   groups prevent repeated versions of one idea from dominating the pool.
5. Human reviewers handle ambiguous or high-risk cases, appeals, and final
   safety policy decisions.
6. Eligible suggestions or clusters receive transparent weights based on
   freshness, category balance, accessibility, prior use, community support,
   and editorial status.
7. A daily or weekly community prompt is selected by weighted random choice
   from the eligible pool.

Random selection makes participation meaningful without turning popularity
into a guaranteed outcome. Weights should be visible at a policy level even
if anti-gaming details are not exposed.

## Prompt categories

Coding is one ordinary category alongside writing, drawing, photography,
animation, audio, video, game design, and other supported media. A coding
prompt should not run arbitrary submitted code by default; submissions are
treated as content unless a separately reviewed execution environment is
later designed.

## Sponsored prompt calendar

Sponsored prompts are separate from the ordinary community prompt pool. They
may use weekly or monthly calendar slots, while daily prompts remain
community-driven.

A sponsor request requires:

- verified company identity and blacklist screening;
- a written prompt brief, audience, content boundaries, deadline, and
  deliverables;
- human manual review before publication;
- clear sponsor labeling;
- prize budget, judging rubric, eligibility, payment terms, taxes, fees, and
  delivery timeline;
- a minimum-valid-submission rule so blank or invalid work cannot win.

Sponsor requests may be considered first-come after review, or placed into a
separate reviewed calendar. Sponsorship must not silently displace normal
prompts or bypass moderation.

## Judging and prizes

A sponsored challenge may use a mixed model: company judging contributes 60%
and community ranking contributes 40%. Eligible top scorers are then
reviewed by the company under the published rubric to select five winners.

The payout formula must be explicit. If the platform takes 15% and payment
processing takes 10% from the same gross amount, 75% remains before taxes,
not 80%. The sponsor may fund fees separately, but the displayed amount must
match the actual calculation.

## Child and age-sensitive content

Ordinary non-sexual fictional depictions of children can be allowed. Sexual
exploitation of minors is prohibited. Age-sensitive prompts require AI
screening, human review when ambiguous, minimum viewing-age labels, and
appropriate audience restrictions.
